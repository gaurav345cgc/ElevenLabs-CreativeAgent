# **GenMyō Own-Content Production Plan** 

_Prepared for Gaurav  —  what we produce ourselves, for whom, at what length, and in what order_ 

## **Purpose of this document** 

We build our practice video library from two sources: we **license** existing high-quality videos where good content already exists, and we **produce our own** where good content cannot be found. This document sets out exactly what we should produce ourselves — the specific wounds to cover for each generation, the ideal video lengths, and a phased order of development so we move quickly while managing cost tightly. 

**A note on the codes:** _wounds are labelled with short codes such as GX-01 or GZ-04. Each combines a generation or situational prefix (GZ Gen Z, ML Millennial, GX Gen X, BO Boomer; WK work, PH physical, FR friendships, RL relationships) with a number, and the full wound name is written out beside every code in the tables below._ 

## **Part 1 — How we chose what to produce** 

For every wound we looked at two things: how many usable licensed videos already exist, and **which video lengths are missing** . A wound with four videos that are all 15–60 minutes still has a gap — it has no short entry-point practice. Two patterns drive the whole plan: 

- **No short content anywhere.** Our shortest usable video today is 10 minutes. Every generation needs 3–6 minute practices, and these are the cheapest to produce. 

- **Three genuinely thin wounds.** GX-01 Invisible Sacrifice (2 videos), BO-04 Disconnection from Body (1 video), and Gen Z short-form — these are the real priorities.

## **Part 3 — Video length, by purpose** 

Length should follow the job the video does, not the generation. Because we are heavily over-supplied with long content already (most licensed videos are 20 minutes or more, and none are under 10 minutes), our own production deliberately skews short — that is where licensing left us exposed and where cost is lowest. 

|**Length band**|**Purpose**|**Share of our own build**|**Best for**|
|---|---|---|---|
|3–6 min (micro)|Entry point, acute regulaton, “I have two<br>minutes”|~30% (12 videos)|Gen Z frst; acute situatonal fares|

|15–20 min (mid)|Deeper seated practce|~25% (10 videos)|Millennial and Gen X core wounds|
|25–30 min (long)|Grief, identty, deep processing|~10% (4 videos)|Boomer and heavy core wounds|

## **Part 5 — What our voice budget means** 

Our voice generation runs on a monthly allowance of **121,000 credits** , which costs about **US $44 per month** in total. Using a safe planning figure of 5,000 credits per five-minute meditation, that allowance covers roughly **24 five-minute meditations every month** . 

|**What we are planning**|**What the budget gives us**|
|---|---|
|Monthly credit allowance|121,000 credits (~US $44/month)|
|Safe cost per fve-minute meditaton|~5,000 credits (~US $1.82 of voice)|
|Five-minute meditatons per month|~24|
|Voice needed for the full ~36-video plan|~399,000 credits (about three monthly cycles)|



**The key implication:** voice is not our constraint — it is nearly free, and the monthly allowance paces almost perfectly onto our three phases (roughly one phase per monthly cycle, with no overage and no plan upgrade needed). Our real cost is the assembly labour, and phasing controls that automatically. In short, the budget does not limit _what_ we build — only how fast we draw it down. 

## **Part 6 — The best way forward** 

We are balancing three pressures: we need to talk to investors, we need content quickly, and we are managing cost tightly. These point to the same move. 

- **Front-load a small, sharp Phase 1 now.** Around eight polished videos in the next credit cycle, produced properly in one consistent GenMyō voice. 

- **It gives us investor proof.** We walk in with real produced content that we own — proof the pipeline exists — rather than a plan. Eight excellent videos beat thirty planned ones in that conversation. 

- **It gives us content quickly.** Micro-content is fastest to script and assemble, and fills our one universal gap (no short videos today). 

- **It manages cost tightly.** Phase 1 uses about a third of one month’s free credit pool and ~$135–265 of assembly. We validate quality before committing anything further. 

**The one discipline that holds it together:** narrow the scope, hold the quality. A rushed first batch would hurt us on both the investor axis (it reads as thin resourcing) and the user axis. Eight excellent videos in one consistent voice — not twenty rushed ones. Phasing is what lets us be fast _and_ cheap without trading away quality.
