**Rationale (condensed, one line each):**

- **WK-06**: Obsolescence is a values/identity crisis about *what still counts as valuable*, not a rest problem — Cognitive Clarity reframes worth away from "current on tools." Stage 3 because the wound needs reflective processing, not just soothing.
- **BO-04**: Disconnection from body is literally a body-awareness wound; the shortest, gentlest re-entry point — Somatic Healing, stage 2 as a first micro touch, not deep work yet.
- **GX-01**: Invisible sacrifice is a *relational* wound (unseen by the people you sacrifice for) as much as an emotional one — Relational Wellbeing centers being witnessed, not just calming down.
- **GZ-04**: Validation dependency is exactly an emotional-regulation skill gap (self-soothing without external input) — matches the acute, post-scroll use case at 3 minutes.
- **ML-03**: Suppressing your authentic self requires *noticing* the gap between performed and real self before anything else — Mindful Awareness is the entry pillar; regulation/purpose come later in the arc.
- **PH-01**: "Living with" pain (not fixing it) is the definition of Somatic Healing done right — explicitly non-medical, body-as-companion framing.
- **WK-04**: Betrayal produces anger/grief that needs regulating before it hardens into cynicism — Emotional Regulation, not Relational Wellbeing, because the wound is about broken trust in a *system*, not a person.
- **WK-05**: Hollow purpose at work is a meaning/values crisis at the level of "why," which is Spiritual Inquiry's territory (the search for meaning) rather than Cognitive Clarity's territory (making sense of change) — this is also the deliberate differentiator from WK-06.
- **PH-03**: Fatigue/burnout in the body is fundamentally a rest-and-energy problem — Deep Rest & Sleep is the direct match, distinct from BO-04's *disconnection* (feeling nothing) and PH-01's *pain* (feeling too much).
- **FR**: Friendship loss for Gen Z is squarely relational — belonging and emotional intelligence about reading the friendship, not fixing it or oneself.

## SECTION B — Relatability Brief Pack

### 1. WK-06 · Age / Tech Obsolescence

**listener_portrait**
Mid-to-late-career, been good at their job for a long time. A younger colleague explains a tool to them in a slightly-too-patient voice. They've started saying "I'm not a tech person" as a pre-emptive shield. They still outperform most of the room on judgment, but judgment isn't what gets praised in meetings anymore. There's a quiet fear under the competence: *if this is what "current" looks like now, when do I stop being useful?*

**wound_story**
Body feel: a tightness across the chest that shows up specifically in meetings where a new system is being rolled out; a held breath before asking a question they're sure is "obvious." Inner monologue: *"I used to be the one people came to."* / *"If I ask again, they'll think I can't keep up."* / *"Maybe this is just what's left for people my age — hang on until they don't need you."* Somatic tells: jaw tightening at "quick sync," shoulders rounding forward at a laptop screen, a reflexive apology before speaking up. Life context: recent reorg, a "digital transformation" initiative, being asked to "shadow" someone half their tenure.

**pillar_feel**
Cognitive Clarity here isn't about learning the new tool — it's about untangling *what actually made them valuable in the first place* from *what a dashboard can now do faster.* The whole session should feel like clearing fog, not hyping them up. Clarity, not confidence theater.

**situational_frame**
- Required life domain: paid work, specifically technology/skills currency at work.
- must_use_phrases: "the tools keep changing," "you've been doing this a long time," "a system that updates overnight," "what you know in your hands," "younger than you in the room," "explaining it slower than you'd like," "still useful," "the parts no software does," "keeping up," "left behind," "the years you put in," "what doesn't show up on a dashboard," "your judgment," "worth isn't a version number."

**must_not_say**
- The three-letter codes or wound_id.
- "You are not obsolete" as a direct denial (too defensive — let the listener arrive there).
- Diagnosing ("this is anxiety," "this is ageism").
- Lecturing about upskilling, courses, LinkedIn Learning, "growth mindset" as advice.
- Medical claims about aging or cognition.
- Bleeding into WK-04 (no betrayal language — nobody did this *to* them) or WK-05 (no "hollow purpose" language — this is about competence, not meaning).

**opening_job**
- Name the specific scene: a new system, a room, a feeling of being slightly behind.
- Do not rush to reassure — let the discomfort be accurately named first.
- Signal this is a private, unjudged space — no one grading them here.
- Orient toward breath/body as the place they can actually rest right now.

**arc_by_section**
- *opening*: Land in the exact scene (a meeting, a screen, a sync) without naming the emotion yet; invite noticing what's here.
- *settling*: Let the body soften from "performing competence" into just sitting; permission to not know something right now.
- *body*: Scan for where "keeping up" lives physically — jaw, shoulders, breath; soften each without fixing them.
- *breath*: Slow the pace deliberately — this pillar wants unhurried thinking, so breath should feel roomy, not brisk.
- *mind_heart*: Separate "current" from "valuable" — years of judgment, pattern-recognition, steadiness are not versioned software.
- *closing*: Carry forward a felt sense that what they know in their hands didn't disappear because a screen changed.

**mind_heart_core**
There is a difference between a system updating and a person becoming outdated — one happens overnight, the other never happens at all. The years spent noticing what a dashboard can't see, steadying a room a screen can't steady, are not a version number waiting to expire. Being asked to learn something new is not the same as being told you no longer matter, even when it feels exactly the same in the body.

**closing_promise**
What you built with your hands and your years is still here, still yours, whatever screen is open next.

**relatability_tests**
1. Does it name a *specific* workplace-technology scene (new system, younger colleague, being shown something) rather than generic "change is hard"? — Yes required.
2. Does it avoid any upskilling/advice language? — Yes required.
3. Would this also pass as WK-04 Organisational Betrayal? → **FAIL** if there's any "they did this to me" / trust-broken language — must stay in the register of *self-doubt about currency*, not *anger at an institution*.
4. Does it separate competence from currency by the mind_heart turn? — Yes required.
5. Could a Gen X listener recognize this as "someone gets what it's like when the whole floor moved and you're still standing where it used to be"? — Yes required.

**gold_opening_example**
There's a moment in that meeting — someone pulls up a new screen, moves through it fast, and you feel a half-second behind before you've even said a word. Maybe you've felt that more than once lately. You don't have to explain it or fix it right now. Just let yourself notice it's here, the way you'd notice weather. Nothing to keep up with in this next little while. Just this room, wherever you're sitting, and a breath that's entirely yours.

**anti_generic_traps**
1. *Failure mode*: Script becomes generic "embrace change" content → *Fix*: anchor every section in the specific sensory scene (a synced screen, a younger voice explaining), never abstract "change."
2. *Failure mode*: Script slides into productivity-coach tone ("here's how to upskill") → *Fix*: ban any forward-looking action advice; this is a felt-sense reframe, not a plan.
3. *Failure mode*: Script collapses into generic "you are enough" affirmation with no work specificity → *Fix*: keep at least one concrete workplace noun (dashboard, sync, reorg, tool, screen) in every section.

### 2. BO-04 · Disconnection from Body

**listener_portrait**
Retired or near-retired, spent decades living "from the neck up" — a career of decisions, meetings, providing. They can tell you what they think about almost anything, but if asked what they *feel*, physically, right now, there's a pause. They eat when it's time, not when hungry. They've noticed, lately, a kind of numbness — not sadness exactly, just distance from their own skin.

**wound_story**
Body feel: a general muted quality, like static between mind and body; noticing the body mostly when it hurts or fails, never otherwise. Inner monologue: *"I don't really know what I feel."* / *"My body's just been along for the ride."* / *"When did I stop being in here?"* Somatic tells: sitting very still without noticing it; shallow breath that's gone unnoticed for years; surprise at the simple sensation of feet on the floor. Life context: retirement, an empty calendar where meetings used to be, a doctor's comment about "just listening to your body," grandchildren who ask "how do you feel, grandpa?" with disarming directness.

**pillar_feel**
Somatic Healing at its most literal and gentle — this is a *first* re-entry, not deep bodywork. The whole session should feel like turning a very quiet dial up one notch, not flooding them with sensation.

**situational_frame**
- Required life domain: the body itself, post-career, aging, retirement.
- must_use_phrases: "from the neck up," "living in your head," "your own skin," "a life of decisions," "the body's been waiting," "feet on the floor," "no meeting to run right now," "quiet static," "coming back," "just for a moment," "no fixing needed," "this body has carried you," "not a project," "here, now, in this body."

**must_not_say**
- Medical claims about numbness, dissociation, or nerve function.
- Diagnosing ("this is depression," "this could be dissociation").
- Any fitness/exercise advice ("try stretching," "walking helps").
- Grief-specific language (that's BO-02) or legacy/role language (BO-01/BO-03).
- The codes.

**opening_job**
- Meet them exactly where they are: mostly in the head, and that's fine.
- No pressure to "feel deeply" — this is a first, small return.
- Frame the body as something that's simply been waiting, not neglected or failing.
- Establish this as unhurried, with nowhere to be.

**arc_by_section**
- *opening*: Acknowledge a life spent mostly in thought and decision; invite curiosity, not correction.
- *settling*: Let the pace slow enough that the body can register at all — this pillar needs generous silence.
- *body*: Gentle, literal scan — feet, hands, the chair — naming sensation without interpreting it.
- *breath*: Breath as the simplest bridge between head and body; nothing technical.
- *mind_heart*: The body isn't a machine that served the mind — it's been a quiet companion the whole time, still here.
- *closing*: A felt sense of having, briefly, come back into the room they never really left.

**mind_heart_core**
A body that's been quiet for a long time isn't a body that stopped working — it's a body that's been waiting to be noticed again, the way an old friend waits without keeping score. There's nothing to fix here, nothing to catch up on. Just this simple fact: you are, this very moment, *in here*, in this body, and it has been carrying you the entire time, asking for nothing.

**closing_promise**
Whatever you noticed just now — however small — is yours to return to any time.

**relatability_tests**
1. Does it use gentle, literal sensation language (feet, hands, chair) rather than emotional excavation? — Yes required.
2. Does it avoid any exercise/movement advice? — Yes required.
3. Would this also pass as PH-03 Fatigue/Burnout? → **FAIL** if there's tiredness/exhaustion language — this is *numbness/distance*, not *depletion*.
4. Does the mind_heart turn frame the body as companion, not machine? — Yes required.
5. Could a Boomer listener recognize "I've lived in my head my whole life" without ever hearing that phrase said aloud? — Yes required.

**gold_opening_example**
For a lot of years, maybe most of them, you've lived mostly up here — in decisions, in plans, in the next thing to handle. That's alright. That's what the years asked of you. But just for these few minutes, there's nowhere to decide and nothing to handle. So let's do something small and unfamiliar. Let's come back down, gently, into the body that's been sitting quietly underneath all that thinking, the whole time, waiting.

**anti_generic_traps**
1. *Failure mode*: Becomes a generic body scan indistinguishable from any sleep meditation → *Fix*: keep the "neck up / disconnection" framing explicit in opening and closing, not just a scan.
2. *Failure mode*: Turns into grief content by accident → *Fix*: no loss language; this is about presence, not absence of someone.
3. *Failure mode*: Sounds like a yoga-class body scan (technical, instructional) → *Fix*: keep language companionable and slow, invite-only, never instructive ("bring your awareness to...").

### 3. GX-01 · Invisible Sacrifice

**listener_portrait**
The one who shows up. Handles the parent's medical appointments, the kid's school forms, the coworker's dropped ball, without being asked and often without being thanked. They've quietly built an identity around being reliable, and they resent, faintly, that reliability reads as "no big deal" to everyone else. They wouldn't call it a sacrifice out loud — that feels like complaining.

**wound_story**
Body feel: a low, constant hum of tension in the shoulders and neck, like carrying something that's never set down. Inner monologue: *"Nobody sees what this costs me."* / *"If I stopped, would anyone notice, or just be inconvenienced?"* / *"I didn't ask for credit. I just wanted to matter to someone the way I make things work for everyone."* Somatic tells: a tight exhale after finishing a task no one commented on; a flash of irritation quickly swallowed; the specific fatigue of being needed but not seen. Life context: aging parents, teenage or grown kids, a job where they're the "glue," a sibling or coworker who gets noticed for less.

**pillar_feel**
Relational Wellbeing here isn't about improving relationships with technique — it's about the listener being *witnessed* by the session itself, so the sacrifice stops being invisible for these few minutes, at least to themselves.

**situational_frame**
- Required life domain: caregiving/reliability roles — family, work, or both — where effort goes unacknowledged.
- must_use_phrases: "the one who shows up," "quietly carried," "no one saw," "you didn't ask for thanks, but," "held it together," "the weight you don't put down," "mattered without being noticed," "what it costs to be reliable," "you kept things running," "seen, just for a moment," "not invisible here," "the parts no one thanked you for."

**must_not_say**
- Codes or wound_id.
- Advice on "setting boundaries" or "asking for help" (that's action-plan territory, not this session's job).
- Resentment-stoking language that hardens into blame of specific people.
- Diagnosing ("this is martyrdom," "codependency").
- Bleeding into WK-04 (this is unnoticed effort, not betrayal) or GX-04 Cynicism.

**opening_job**
- Name the specific pattern of showing up without being asked.
- Validate that the tiredness is real and earned, not imagined.
- Make clear this space itself is one place the effort is seen.
- Signal there's nothing to perform or manage here.

**arc_by_section**
- *opening*: Land on the felt sense of being the reliable one, quietly, without drama.
- *settling*: Let the body register that nothing is being asked of them right now — a rare state.
- *body*: Locate where the "holding" lives — shoulders, jaw, chest — and let it be witnessed, not fixed.
- *breath*: A breath that's entirely for them, not in service of anyone else.
- *mind_heart*: Being unseen by others doesn't erase what was actually carried — the weight was real even when uncounted.
- *closing*: A small internal acknowledgment that stands in for the thanks that didn't come.

**mind_heart_core**
Nobody kept a ledger of what you carried, and that's exactly why it's felt so light to everyone but you. But weight doesn't need a witness to be real — the appointments made, the calls taken, the things quietly fixed before anyone noticed they were broken, all of it happened, all of it counted, whether or not anyone said so. For this one moment, let it be counted here.

**closing_promise**
What you've carried is real, even on the days no one says so.

**relatability_tests**
1. Does it name unpaid, unacknowledged effort specifically (not just "you're tired")? — Yes required.
2. Does it avoid boundary-setting or "ask for help" advice? — Yes required.
3. Would this also pass as WK-04 Organisational Betrayal? → **FAIL** if it implies someone actively wronged them — this is about being *overlooked*, not *betrayed*.
4. Does the mind_heart turn validate the weight without needing external witness to justify it? — Yes required.
5. Could a Gen X listener recognize "I'm the one who holds it all together and no one notices" without those exact words? — Yes required.

**gold_opening_example**
You handled it again — whatever it was this week. The call nobody else made, the thing that would've fallen apart if you hadn't quietly caught it. No one clapped. No one probably even knew. That's alright. You don't need an audience for this next part. Just you, this breath, and a few minutes where nothing needs to be carried, held, or fixed by anyone — including you.

**anti_generic_traps**
1. *Failure mode*: Turns into generic "you deserve rest" content with no specificity → *Fix*: keep at least one concrete caregiving/reliability scene per section.
2. *Failure mode*: Slides into resentment/blame of named people ("your family doesn't appreciate you") → *Fix*: keep focus on the listener's internal experience, never indict specific others.
3. *Failure mode*: Becomes advice-y ("learn to say no") → *Fix*: this session witnesses, it does not coach; no action steps.

### 4. GZ-04 · Validation Dependency

**listener_portrait**
Just put the phone down after checking a post one more time. Knows, intellectually, that a number shouldn't decide their mood, but it does, for the next hour at least. There's a specific anxious loop: post, wait, check, compare, feel the dip if it's quiet. They're not vain — they're exhausted from needing proof, constantly, that they're okay.

**wound_story**
Body feel: a jittery, unsettled energy in the chest and hands right after checking; a small crash when the numbers don't come. Inner monologue: *"Did anyone see it?"* / *"Why do I care this much?"* / *"I know this isn't real, but it feels real."* Somatic tells: the reflexive hand-check for the phone; a held breath while a page loads; relief or deflation that arrives faster than thought. Life context: right after posting, right after a low-engagement post, mid-scroll comparison spiral, the specific comedown of closing an app.

**pillar_feel**
Emotional Regulation, fast-acting and small — this is a 3-minute *reset*, not deep exploration. The pillar should feel like a hand on the shoulder right after the crash, not a workshop on self-esteem.

**situational_frame**
- Required life domain: social media / online validation-seeking, specifically the post-scroll or post-check moment.
- must_use_phrases: "just closed the app," "checked it again," "the number," "waiting to see," "didn't feel like enough," "still here without it," "the quiet after scrolling," "worth isn't a count," "before you even opened it," "your own approval," "this feeling will pass," "you don't need proof right now."

**must_not_say**
- Codes.
- Lecturing about "social media is bad" or screen-time advice.
- Shaming ("you shouldn't care so much").
- Diagnosing ("this is social media addiction").
- Bleeding into ML-03 (self-suppression) — this is about *seeking* validation, not *hiding* the self.

**opening_job**
- Land immediately in the post-scroll, post-check moment — no warm-up needed at 3 minutes.
- Normalize the feeling without judgment.
- Offer an immediate, small point of regulation (breath, body).
- Keep pacing brisk but calm — a reset, not a lull.

**arc_by_section**
- *opening*: Name the exact moment — just checked, just posted, just scrolled — and the flat or jittery feeling after.
- *settling*: A quick landing in the body, away from the screen's pace.
- *body*: One or two sensations only (chest, hands) — this is too short for a full scan.
- *breath*: Very short, a few breaths, deliberately slower than scrolling pace.
- *mind_heart*: The gap between the self that exists before any number and the self that needs the number — and a small return to the former.
- *closing*: A brief, portable phrase they can carry back into the day.

**mind_heart_core**
Before you ever opened the app, you were already whole — that part of you doesn't wait for a number to confirm it exists. The dip you feel right now isn't proof you're not okay; it's just proof that a small machine designed to make you check again did exactly what it was built to do. You don't need it to agree with you for you to be fine.

**closing_promise**
You're still here, still whole, with or without the count.

**relatability_tests**
1. Does it open directly in the post-scroll/post-check moment (no slow build)? — Yes required.
2. Does it avoid screen-time lecturing? — Yes required.
3. Would this also pass as ML-03 Authentic Self Suppression? → **FAIL** if it's about hiding a true self rather than needing external proof of worth.
4. Is the whole arc short and brisk enough to fit 3 minutes without feeling rushed or cut? — Yes required.
5. Could a Gen Z listener recognize the specific "just closed the app and feel weirdly deflated" moment? — Yes required.

**gold_opening_example**
You just checked. Maybe more than once. And whatever number was there, it didn't quite land the way you hoped. That flat, jittery feeling right now — that's real, and it's okay that it's here. But before any of that even happened, you were already fine. Let's find that part again, just for a minute.

**anti_generic_traps**
1. *Failure mode*: Turns into a lecture about social media harm → *Fix*: zero meta-commentary on platforms; stay inside the felt experience only.
2. *Failure mode*: Too long/slow for a 3-minute micro (feels like a cut-down 10-minute script) → *Fix*: write for 3 minutes natively — one sensation, a few breaths, one reframe, done.
3. *Failure mode*: Sounds preachy ("your worth doesn't come from likes") stated as a command → *Fix*: phrase as gentle noticing/invitation, never as correction.

### 5. ML-03 · Authentic Self Suppression

**listener_portrait**
Says the agreeable thing in the meeting, the group chat, the family dinner — even when it costs them something internally. Has gotten so good at reading the room and adjusting that they sometimes lose track of what they'd say if no one were listening. Successful by most outside measures. Quietly wonders who they'd be if they stopped performing competence and likability at the same time.

**wound_story**
Body feel: a subtle tightness in the throat right before saying the agreeable thing instead of the true thing; a low fatigue that isn't physical, more like the tiredness of translation. Inner monologue: *"That's not really what I think."* / *"If I said the real thing, would they still want me here?"* / *"I don't even know anymore which version is 'me.'"* Somatic tells: a practiced, easy smile that doesn't reach further than the mouth; nodding before fully agreeing; a small relief when finally alone. Life context: work meetings, group chats, family gatherings, the specific millennial habit of curating a palatable self across contexts.

**pillar_feel**
Mindful Awareness — not "be your true self" advice, but simply *noticing* the gap between the performed self and the one underneath, without judgment or immediate pressure to change anything.

**situational_frame**
- Required life domain: social/professional performance of self — meetings, group settings, family, online persona.
- must_use_phrases: "the agreeable thing," "not quite what you meant," "reading the room," "which version of you," "the smile that doesn't reach," "alone, finally," "underneath the performance," "no one's watching right now," "what you'd actually say," "tired of translating," "just noticing, not changing," "the real answer."

**must_not_say**
- Codes.
- Advice to "just be yourself" or "speak your truth" as a directive/command.
- Career advice (quitting, confronting people, having "the conversation").
- Diagnosing ("this is people-pleasing," "this is masking").
- Bleeding into GZ-04 (this is about hiding truth, not seeking validation).

**opening_job**
- Land in a specific moment of saying the agreeable rather than true thing.
- Frame this purely as noticing, with zero pressure to change anything today.
- Offer relief that this space requires no performance at all.
- Set a tone of curiosity, not confrontation.

**arc_by_section**
- *opening*: A specific scene — a meeting, a chat, a dinner — and the small internal edit that happened before speaking.
- *settling*: Let the performing muscles (face, posture, voice) rest, since nobody's grading this.
- *body*: Notice where "translation" or performance lives physically — jaw, throat, shoulders.
- *breath*: Breath as the one thing that doesn't need editing or adjusting for an audience.
- *mind_heart*: Distinguish between adapting (skillful, human) and disappearing (costly) — noticing which one has been happening.
- *closing*: A quiet, private permission to know the true answer, even if it's not spoken aloud today.

**mind_heart_core**
There's a difference between adjusting how you say something and losing track of what you actually think — one is a skill, the other is a slow disappearing act. You don't have to say the real thing out loud right now, to anyone. But just for these few minutes, you're allowed to *know* it, privately, without editing it down to something more comfortable for the room.

**closing_promise**
Whatever you actually think is still in there, still yours, whether or not today is the day you say it.

**relatability_tests**
1. Does it land in a specific social/professional performance scene rather than abstract "be authentic" talk? — Yes required.
2. Does it avoid telling the listener to confront anyone or change their behavior today? — Yes required.
3. Would this also pass as GZ-04 Validation Dependency? → **FAIL** if it's about needing others' approval rather than losing track of one's own true view.
4. Does the mind_heart turn distinguish adapting from disappearing? — Yes required.
5. Could a Millennial listener recognize "I said the agreeable thing again and I'm not sure I remember what I actually think" without hearing it verbatim? — Yes required.

**gold_opening_example**
Somewhere today — a meeting, a chat, a dinner table — you probably said the version of the thing that would land easiest. Maybe you do that so often it barely registers anymore. There's no need to change that right now, no conversation to have with anyone else. Just for these few minutes, with no one reading the room, let's see what's actually underneath.

**anti_generic_traps**
1. *Failure mode*: Becomes generic "find your true self" content with no situational grounding → *Fix*: keep at least one concrete social scene (meeting, chat, dinner) per section.
2. *Failure mode*: Pushes toward action ("tell them how you feel") → *Fix*: this is a noticing session; explicitly no behavior-change instruction.
3. *Failure mode*: Sounds like it's shaming people-pleasing → *Fix*: frame adapting to others as a skill, not a flaw — the wound is the *cost*, not the adaptation itself.

### 6. PH-01 · Chronic Pain

**listener_portrait**
Lives with pain that doesn't fully go away — some days quieter, some days loud. Has had the well-meaning "have you tried..." conversation too many times. Has built a complicated relationship with their own body: it's the thing that hurts, but it's also just... them. They're not looking to be fixed in this moment. **wound_story**

**pillar_feel**

**arc_by_section**

- *settling*: Let go of the vigilance/bracing that chronic pain often requires, just slightly, just here.

- *breath*: Breath as something the pain can't take away, a steady resource regardless of pain level today.
- *mind_heart*: Shift from "body as enemy" to "body as a whole that includes but isn't only the pain."
- *closing*: A felt sense of having, briefly, not fought anything.

**mind_heart_core**

But the body carrying that pain is also the same body that can still breathe, still rest, still notice a patch of quiet somewhere in it right now. **closing_promise**
Whatever the pain is doing today, you're still whole, still here, still worth this quiet.

**relatability_tests**

**gold_opening_example**
It's still here today — maybe quieter, maybe not. Either way, you don't have to fight it for the next few minutes, and you don't have to pretend it isn't there either. This isn't about making it disappear. It's about finding a little room, right alongside it, for you.

**anti_generic_traps**

### 7. WK-04 · Organisational Betrayal

**listener_portrait**
Gave years to an organization that, in some concrete way, didn't return the loyalty — a layoff after being told they were "essential," a promise broken, a leadership decision that revealed the relationship was always more transactional than they'd let themselves believe. **wound_story**

Life context: layoff, demotion, broken promise, restructuring, a leader who said one thing and did another.

**pillar_feel**
Emotional Regulation aimed specifically at anger and grief that have a legitimate target (an institution/decision), not at self-blame — the session should help the feelings move through rather than harden into permanent cynicism.

**situational_frame**
- Required life domain: workplace/organizational trust breach — layoff, broken promise, leadership decision.

**must_not_say**
- Codes.

- Minimizing ("everything happens for a reason," "it wasn't that bad").
- Diagnosing ("this is trauma").
- Bleeding into GX-01 (unnoticed sacrifice, no active wrongdoer) or WK-06 (obsolescence, no betrayal) or WK-05 (hollow purpose, no specific broken trust event).

**opening_job**
- Name the specific kind of breach (a decision, a broken promise) without minimizing.
- Validate anger and grief both as legitimate, not just one or the other.
- Make clear there's no requirement to "get over it" quickly here.
- Signal this space allows the full feeling, contained safely.

**arc_by_section**
- *opening*: Land in the specific moment trust broke — a meeting, an email, a decision — honestly.
- *settling*: Let the body hold the anger without needing to act on it right now.
- *body*: Locate where betrayal lives physically — chest, jaw, throat — and let it be acknowledged.
- *breath*: Breath as a way to metabolize the heat of anger without suppressing it.
- *mind_heart*: Separate what the institution did from what it says about the listener's worth or judgment.
- *closing*: A sense that this doesn't have to become permanent cynicism — the feeling moved, even slightly.

**mind_heart_core**
Believing in something that turns out not to have believed in you back isn't naivety — it's what trust looks like before it's tested. What happened says something true about a decision made by people protecting themselves; it doesn't say anything true about your judgment, your effort, or your worth. The anger gets to be here. So does the grief. Neither one has to calcify into permanent distrust of every door from now on.

**closing_promise**
What broke was their promise, not your judgment for having believed it.

**relatability_tests**

2. Does it validate anger without pushing toward retaliation or legal/career advice? — Yes required.
3. Would this also pass as GX-01 Invisible Sacrifice? → **FAIL** if there's no specific breach — GX-01 is about *unnoticed* effort, not *broken trust*.

5. Would this also pass as WK-05 Purpose at Work? → **FAIL** if it's about hollow meaning rather than a concrete trust violation.

**gold_opening_example**

**anti_generic_traps**

### 9. PH-03 · Fatigue / Burnout

**listener_portrait**
Tired in a way sleep doesn't fully fix. Has been running on a kind of low-grade depletion for months, maybe longer — still functioning, still showing up, but running on the reserve tank. Has probably said "I'm just tired" so many times it's stopped meaning anything to the people around them, and maybe to themselves too.

**wound_story**
Body feel: a heaviness in the limbs and eyes that a full night's sleep doesn't resolve; a flatness in enthusiasm for things that used to feel easy. Inner monologue: *"I don't remember the last time I felt actually rested."* / *"I keep saying I'm fine because what else is there to say?"* / *"When did tired become the default?"* Somatic tells: needing caffeine just to reach baseline, not "boost"; heavy eyelids in the middle of ordinary tasks; a short fuse that surprises them. Life context: an extended period of overextension — caregiving, work, life demands stacked without real recovery — where "tired" has become a chronic state rather than an occasional one.

**pillar_feel**
Deep Rest & Sleep, but as *permission* to actually rest rather than a sleep-hygiene tutorial — the pillar should feel like exhaling for the first time in a long while.

**situational_frame**
- Required life domain: chronic physical/energetic depletion, not occasional tiredness and not emotional numbness.
- must_use_phrases: "tired that sleep doesn't fix," "running on the reserve tank," "just tired, for a long time now," "permission to actually rest," "not a failure to keep up," "the weight in your limbs," "more than one bad night," "rest isn't a reward you have to earn," "the body asking to stop," "nowhere to be for a few minutes."

**must_not_say**
- Codes.
- Medical claims about sleep disorders or chronic fatigue conditions.
- Productivity framing ("rest so you can perform better").
- Diagnosing ("this is burnout syndrome," "this is depression").
- Bleeding into BO-04 (numbness/disconnection) or PH-01 (pain) — this is specifically *depletion*, heaviness, low energy.

**opening_job**
- Name the specific, chronic quality of the tiredness (not a bad night — a long stretch).
- Validate that "I'm fine" has been covering for something real.
- Frame rest as permission, not a productivity tool.
- Lower the pace immediately — this pillar wants an early exhale.

**arc_by_section**
- *opening*: Land in the chronic, sleep-doesn't-fix-it quality of the fatigue honestly.
- *settling*: An early, deliberate exhale — permission to stop performing "fine."
- *body*: Locate the heaviness — limbs, eyes, chest — and let it be acknowledged as real, not weak.
- *breath*: Slow, heavy-feeling breath that matches and honors the depletion rather than energizing past it.
- *mind_heart*: Rest reframed as something the body is owed, not something earned through more output.
- *closing*: A felt sense of having actually rested for a few minutes, not just paused.

**mind_heart_core**
This tiredness isn't a personal failing or a productivity problem to optimize away — it's a body that has been running long past empty, doing exactly what a body does under that kind of sustained demand. Rest was never something you had to earn by finishing everything first. For these few minutes, there's nothing left to prove and nowhere left to be — just permission, finally, to actually stop.

**closing_promise**
You don't have to earn this rest — it's already yours.

**relatability_tests**
1. Does it name chronic, sleep-resistant fatigue specifically, not a single bad night? — Yes required.
2. Does it avoid productivity-framed rest ("rest to perform better")? — Yes required.
3. Would this also pass as BO-04 Disconnection from Body? → **FAIL** if it's about numbness/distance rather than heaviness/depletion.
4. Would this also pass as PH-01 Chronic Pain? → **FAIL** if there's no pain sensation, only tiredness.
5. Could someone in ongoing burnout feel "someone finally said I don't have to earn my rest"? — Yes required.

**gold_opening_example**
This isn't the kind of tired one good night fixes. It's been longer than that — weeks, maybe longer — of running on whatever's left in the tank. You don't have to explain it or push through it right now. There's nothing left to finish before you're allowed to rest. Just this: permission, finally, to actually stop.

**anti_generic_traps**
1. *Failure mode*: Becomes generic sleep-hygiene content (screens before bed, routines) → *Fix*: zero sleep-tips content; this is permission and presence, not technique.
2. *Failure mode*: Frames rest as instrumental to future performance → *Fix*: never justify rest by what it enables later; rest is the point, not the fuel.
3. *Failure mode*: Indistinguishable from a generic "relax" meditation → *Fix*: keep the chronic, cumulative nature of the fatigue explicit ("weeks," "longer than that," "sleep doesn't fix it").

### 10. FR · Friendship Overlays (Loneliness / Friendship Loss)

**listener_portrait**
Watched friendships that used to feel effortless quietly fade — not a fight, not a falling out, just distance that crept in through moves, jobs, life stages, and unanswered texts that eventually stopped being sent. Feels this most sharply at specific moments — a birthday with fewer texts than expected, a scroll through old photos.

**wound_story**

**pillar_feel**
Relational Wellbeing, specifically grief-adjacent — this is mourning a friendship's fade without pathologizing it, and reconnecting the listener to their own capacity for connection rather than "fixing" the specific lost friendship.

**situational_frame**
- Required life domain: friendship specifically — not romantic relationships, not family — loss, drift, or fade of platonic connection.

- Validate the grief as real, even without a clear villain or clear ending.

**arc_by_section**

- *breath*: Breath as steady company for a feeling that often shows up alone.
- *mind_heart*: Drift isn't the same as being unlovable — friendships end for reasons that aren't verdicts on worth.
- *closing*: A quiet reminder that the capacity for closeness that made that friendship real is still intact.

**mind_heart_core**
A friendship fading through distance and time isn't the same story as being hard to love — most friendships end this way, quietly, without a villain, without a single moment you could point to and fix. The ache is real grief, even without a clear ending to grieve. And the part of you that knew how to be close to someone, that built that friendship in the first place — that part didn't disappear along with the friendship. It's still here, still capable, whenever you're ready.

**closing_promise**
The part of you that knows how to be close to someone is still here, still yours.

**relatability_tests**

2. Does it avoid romantic-relationship language entirely? — Yes required.
3. Would this also pass as GZ-02 Belonging Without Roots? → **FAIL** if it's about general rootlessness/identity rather than a specific faded friendship or friendships.
4. Does it avoid telling the listener to reach out as an instruction? — Yes required.
5. Could a Gen Z listener recognize "we used to be close and I don't even know when that stopped being true" without hearing it verbatim? — Yes required.

**gold_opening_example**
Maybe it was an old photo, or a group chat that's gone quiet, or a birthday with fewer messages than it used to have. ## SECTION E — Generation Register Kits

### Gen Z (gen_z)
**Do's**: Speak in short, direct sentences; reference digital/social scenes concretely (the app, the scroll, the group chat); validate feelings without minimizing; keep sessions brief and punchy for shorts/micros; treat self-awareness ("I know this isn't rational, but...") as already present, don't lecture it in.
**Don'ts**: Don't use dated slang or try to sound "young"; don't moralize about screen time; don't assume naivety — this generation is often more self-aware about their patterns than older content assumes; don't over-explain digital culture as if foreign.
**5 relatability markers**: (1) immediate acknowledgment that feelings can be "known to be irrational" and still real, (2) concrete digital-scene grounding (app, screen, scroll, notification), (3) brevity — doesn't need long buildups to trust the content, (4) comfort naming internal contradiction directly, (5) skepticism toward generic positivity — needs specificity to feel real.

**"Too Boomer/mismatched" sounds like**: Treating phones/social media as inherently bad, assuming the listener needs basic explanation of digital culture, pacing that's too slow/formal for a generation used to fast, dense information, moralizing tone about "kids these days."

### Millennial (millennial)
**Do's**: Acknowledge the specific exhaustion of "having it together" on the outside; reference concrete adult-life scenes (meetings, group chats, family dinners, curated online presence); validate the gap between performance and authenticity; use language that respects competence while naming its cost.
**Don'ts**: Don't assume crisis or failure — millennials in this wound-set are often outwardly successful; don't push toward dramatic life change (quit the job, leave the relationship); don't be preachy about authenticity as a moral requirement.
**5 relatability markers**: (1) tension between outward competence and inward uncertainty, (2) awareness of performing across multiple contexts (work self, family self, online self), (3) fatigue that isn't dramatic — a low hum, not a crisis, (4) self-aware irony about self-help/wellness culture itself — avoid sounding like a listicle, (5) value placed on authenticity as an ideal, even while struggling to live it.
**"Trying too hard" sounds like**: Overusing therapy-speak ("holding space," "doing the work") without grounding it in a specific scene; sounding like a wellness-brand Instagram caption.
**"Too Boomer/mismatched" sounds like**: Assuming stability/settledness that millennials often don't have (mortgage, single steady career); assuming they haven't already tried therapy/self-help — this generation is often over-saturated with self-improvement content, so genuine specificity matters more than general encouragement.

### Gen X (gen_x)
**Do's**: Respect self-reliance as a value, not a flaw to fix; use understated, non-dramatic language; reference concrete work/caregiving scenes; acknowledge being "the reliable one" or "the one who handles it" without requiring them to unlearn that identity entirely; keep tone steady and low-key, not effusive.
**Don'ts**: Don't be saccharine or overly emotional in tone; don't push toward asking for help or "opening up" as an easy fix; don't assume they want validation the way younger generations might — Gen X often prefers being *seen* over being *praised*.
**5 relatability markers**: (1) quiet self-reliance, often to a fault, (2) mild but real cynicism toward institutions and easy answers, (3) being the unacknowledged "glue" in family or work systems, (4) discomfort with vulnerability language that feels too therapy-culture, (5) dry, understated emotional expression rather than dramatic language.
**"Trying too hard" sounds like**: Overly warm, effusive, therapy-speak-heavy language ("let's hold space for your inner child"); pushing emotional vulnerability too hard, too fast.
**"Too Boomer/mismatched" sounds like**: Assuming institutional loyalty is still intact or unwavering; ignoring the specific cynicism this generation has earned; assuming they haven't already tried to just "power through" — that's often the starting point, not the destination.

### Boomer (boomer)
**Do's**: Use unhurried, spacious pacing; respect a lifetime of decision-making and responsibility; reference concrete life-stage scenes (retirement, grandchildren, a body that's changed); frame rest and body-awareness as new territory, not a return to something familiar; keep language dignified, not diminutive.
**Don'ts**: Don't use youth-coded slang or overly casual tone; don't patronize about technology, aging, or health; don't rush the pacing — this generation often benefits from more spaciousness and repetition than younger cohorts; don't assume familiarity with meditation jargon.
**5 relatability markers**: (1) a lifetime spent in decision-making/provider roles, now shifting, (2) unfamiliarity with turning attention inward — this may be genuinely new territory, (3) dignity and self-respect as central values — avoid anything that feels condescending, (4) direct, plain language over trendy wellness vocabulary, (5) comfort with unhurried pacing and silence.
**"Trying too hard" sounds like**: Overusing modern wellness jargon ("somatic release," "nervous system regulation") without translation into plain language; rushing pacing to match shorter-attention-span content norms.
**"Too Gen Z/mismatched" sounds like**: Casual, clipped sentence style; digital-culture references; a rushed pace better suited to a 3-minute micro than a spacious longer session; treating vulnerability as effortless rather than a genuinely new practice for this generation.
